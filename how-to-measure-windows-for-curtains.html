How to measure windows for curtains in Hyderabad.

Two numbers are all a curtain really needs — how wide the rod is, and how far the fabric should fall. Here is how to take both with a tape measure and five minutes, and what to do if you would rather we took them for you.

First rule

Measure the rod, not the glass.

The most common mistake in curtain measuring is measuring the window. Curtains do not hang on the window — they hang on the rod or track above it, and that rod is almost always wider than the frame, so the open curtains can stack back against the wall and leave the glass clear. So your width number is the length of the rod between the finials, or the full length of the track.

No rod up yet? Measure the window frame, add 15 to 20 cm on each side, and that is the width to tell us. It puts the rod where it should be and lets the curtains clear the glass when open.

The two numbers

Width across the top, drop down the side.

One tape across the rod gives you the width. One tape from the rod down to where the fabric should stop gives you the drop. Write both in centimetres, window by window — no two windows in a flat are ever quite the same.

The sketch shows both lines on a typical window. If reading it makes you want someone else to hold the tape, that is what we are for — the shop measures free of charge.

Step by step

Four steps, five minutes a window.

  1. Measure the width of the rod or track

    End to end, finial to finial — not the glass. No rod yet? Frame width plus 15–20 cm each side.

  2. Decide the fullness

    Curtains are sewn wider than the rod so they fall in folds. As a rule of thumb, fabric width comes to about twice the rod width; tell us the look you want — flat and modern, or deep and full — and we cut to suit.

  3. Measure the drop

    From the top of the rod (or the bottom of the track) down to where the fabric should stop: at the sill, about 15 cm below the sill, or just off the floor.

  4. Note the obstructions

    Anything the fabric must clear — a radiator, a study table under the sill, a balcony door that opens inward, an AC unit beside the frame. A phone photo of the window helps us more than a sketch.

Choosing the drop

Sill, below sill, or floor?

Floor length is the default for living rooms and bedrooms — the fabric hangs straight, the room looks taller, and a blackout lining seals the light at the bottom. Stop the drop about a centimetre off the floor so the hem does not sweep dust.

Sill length earns its place where something lives under the window — a kitchen counter, a study table, a radiator. Go 15 cm below the sill when the fabric needs to hang clear of what is beneath it. If the window is a hard one, measure anyway and bring the numbers to the floor; the consultation is free, and our tailors alter the drop if it is a finger off.

Questions

Asked across the counter, answered here.

Will you measure my windows for me?

Yes, and it costs nothing. Call or WhatsApp the shop and we will come home and measure — most customers skip the tape measure entirely and leave it to the same team that stitches and hangs the curtains.

Which drop should I choose — sill, below sill, or floor?

Floor length suits most living rooms and bedrooms — the curtain hangs straight and the room looks taller. Stop at the sill only where something sits under the window, like a study table or a kitchen counter, and go about 15 cm below the sill when a radiator or cabinet needs the fabric to hang clear of it. If you are not sure, we will tell you at the window.

Do I need measurements before visiting the shop?

No. Rough numbers help us quote faster, but they are not required — walk in with nothing but a photo of the window and we will still sort you out, and we measure free if you would rather we did it.
